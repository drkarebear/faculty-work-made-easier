The work behind the work

Teaching is only part of faculty work. There is also a surprising amount of searching, decoding, comparing, troubleshooting, translating institutional language, rebuilding resources, and figuring out systems that were never designed to be intuitive. I build these projects to make some of that hidden labor smaller.

About the author

I am Karen Crozer, a California Community College English faculty member who builds independent tools for faculty and students. Most of these projects began the same way: I was trying to complete a real task, realized the process was harder or more opaque than it needed to be, and started building the resource I wished already existed.

My projects sit at the intersection of teaching, open education, faculty development, accessibility, data, and responsible uses of AI. I am especially interested in reducing the invisible work surrounding good teaching: finding the right information, locating usable course materials, understanding institutional systems, scaffolding learning, and turning complicated processes into something a busy faculty member can actually use.

Faculty Work Made Easier is not a company or an official system office project. It is a home for independent faculty projects I create and maintain because I think useful things should be easier to find, understand, adapt, and share.

Useful before impressive The point is to solve a real problem clearly, not to make a complicated tool look sophisticated.
Faculty judgment stays central AI can help with first-pass work, but consequential decisions still belong to people.
Access is part of design Accessibility, readable language, keyboard use, responsive design, and reuse are built in rather than added later.

How I maintain these projects

These are living resources. I revise them as tools, policies, course structures, accessibility expectations, and faculty needs change.

Human review

I use my own professional judgment to review, revise, and contextualize project content. When AI supports development, I treat it as a tool—not as an authority.

Accessibility

I aim for WCAG 2.2 AA practices, including semantic structure, keyboard access, visible focus, readable contrast, responsive reflow, reduced-motion support, and descriptive links.

Corrections

If you find an error, broken link, confusing explanation, or accessibility problem, I want to know. Independent projects get better when people can point out what needs attention.

Reuse

Where licensing permits, I want faculty to be able to adapt useful materials rather than rebuild them from scratch. Individual project pages explain their licensing and reuse terms.
